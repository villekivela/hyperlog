# Chest-supported T-bar row machine (`t_bar_row_machine`)

Plate-loaded lever with a low rear pivot. Not the seated `row_machine` (laid out as a Gymleco 010, a sit-down row with a vertical chest pad): here the user stands on a foot plate and lies prone on an inclined chest pad.

## Movement and exercises served
The user stands on the foot plate, leans onto the inclined chest pad, and pulls the handles toward the chest. The handles sit on a yoke halfway along a lever that pivots low at the rear, by the foot plate; plates go on a horn at the front end of the lever. At rest the lever lies on a catch post near the front. Chest support takes the lower back out of the lift. The same setup serves the Kelso shrug: chest on the pad, arms near straight, squeeze the shoulder blades together (scapular retraction), not an upward shrug.
- `chest_supported_t_bar_row` (video, titled "Chest Supported T Bar Row": https://youtu.be/160n9FBX84s, viewed at 0:00 to 0:16). A plate-loaded machine seen from the front and a little to the side: chest pad, foot step, horn at the front.
- `kelso_shrug` (the owner does it chest-supported on this type of machine; video, titled "Kelso Shrug": https://youtu.be/76rz0UNAlYI, viewed at 0:00 to 0:20, side-on from 0:02 to 0:10). A lying T-bar machine with a lower pad, the lever pivoting by the feet and the horn at the front.
- `t_bar_row` (video, titled "T Bar Row": https://youtu.be/ZOg0CcJCKrk, viewed at 0:00 to 0:11, three-quarter view): the same kind of machine, not a landmine and not an unsupported T-bar. The user stands on a step with the shins against rollers, chest on an inclined pad, rows a lever whose horn is at the front. The pose (#188) holds the wide grips, overhand, the nearest this machine has to the video's wide handles.

## Motion
- One lever pivoting low at the rear, about 420 mm above the floor at the top of the rear post (photos 01 and 07). The handles sit on a yoke about 780 mm ahead of the pivot and 370 mm above it (an 860 mm radius); the horn is the lever's front end, about 1620 mm ahead of it. All three videos show the same layout: the lever runs from by the feet, under the pad, to the horn at the front.
- The handles travel on an arc up and back toward the chest: a fixed arc, not a free bar.
- Axis: horizontal, across the machine, at the rear.
- Range of motion, row: the lever turns about 23 degrees, from 3 degrees below horizontal (on its catch) to 20 degrees above. Estimated from photos 02 and 03 (same camera), scaled by the 450 mm bumper: the plate's centre rises about 500 mm at a 1300 mm radius. That is about 345 mm of handle travel along the 860 mm arc: about 270 mm up and 210 mm back. Rough, the camera is three-quarter on.
- Range of motion, Kelso shrug: the hands rise about 100 mm with the arms near straight. Estimated from the Kelso video at 0:07 (top) and 0:08 (bottom), measured from the pad's top end, scaled by the plate taken as 450 mm; rough, the camera pans between the frames.
- The horn is a 50 mm sleeve for Olympic plates, 370 mm long (Titan page).

## Handles
- Neutral: 230 mm long, 445 mm apart, one on each upright's block, angled down and forward (away from the user).
- Wide: horizontal grips, 140 mm long, 660 mm apart.
- Both are fixed on the Titan lever's yoke; 31 mm rubber.
- Photos 05 and 06 and the maker's three-quarter photo show two grips on the block atop each yoke upright: a neutral one angled down and forward, and a wide one straight out, none pointing up or back; the recipe (#176) builds them so, the palms upright on the neutral grip, held on its rear half.

## Adjustments and fit rules
- Foot plate: pop-pin, sliding along the inclined beam (photo 10). The page names no other adjustment and the photos show the pad bolted to the beam and post, so the pad is fixed.
- Fit, as the videos and photos show it: set the foot plate so the pad's top end sits at the top of the sternum, the shoulders and head clear of it; the arms then hang straight to the handles with the lever on its catch. The knees are a little bent (all three videos and photo 07).
- Row: the elbows travel back past the torso and the handles come to the lower chest at the pad (photos 03 and 08, the T-bar row video at 0:02 (top) and 0:07 (bottom)).
- Kelso shrug: the same setup; the arms stay near straight and the shoulder blades pull together. At the top the elbows bend a little (Kelso video at 0:07).
- Fit range: 4 ft 11 in to 6 ft 6 in (Titan).
- Placard rules: not found.

## Open gaps
- Owner's machine: its make is unknown and stays so. Titan stands in for the type. The `t_bar_row` video's machine has shin rollers and a step where Titan has an inclined foot plate; the recipe follows Titan.
- Foot plate setting for the mannequin: solved from the fit rule (#188), the pad's top end at the top of the sternum and the knees bent to 160°, which puts the foot plate's centre 913 mm along the beam below the pad's bottom end. In photo 07 it is about 950 mm (estimated, scaled by the 450 mm bumper, rough: the pad's bottom end is hidden by the user's hip). The 160° is the videos' lifters; photo 07's knees are straighter.
- Top of the row: the lever's 20 degrees comes from a three-quarter photo, so the top handle height (about 1060 mm) is rough. The side-on photo 07 shows only the bottom.
- Kelso shrug travel: the frames pan, so the 100 mm is rough.
