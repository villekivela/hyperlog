# Pec deck / rear delt combo (`pec_deck`)

## Movement and exercises served
Seated horizontal adduction (pec fly) on two lever arms that swing in a horizontal plane about vertical axes overhead, loaded by one weight stack. The same machine serves the rear-delt fly (horizontal abduction): the user straddles the same seat facing the back pad, chest on it. The seat does not turn and nothing is adjusted between the two (Hammer Strength demo video at 0:33 to 0:35; the page says the handle placement removes adjusting between exercises).
- `pec_deck`: seated, back on the pad, hands on the vertical grips beside the shoulders, arms sweep together in front of the chest.
- `reverse_pec_deck`: chest on the pad, arms forward to the short level grips, arms sweep apart to the sides.
- `1_arm_reverse_pec_deck`: one arm at a time, sat sideways across the seat as its video shows, on another machine (below). The kit keeps the seat where the owner set it, the pad on the lifter's left: the right arm works the right lever and the left hand lies on the thigh. The right shoulder stands 0.39 m off that lever's axis, so the arm starts long and ends bent, where the video's stays straight (owner, #241).

One machine, no variants: which grip the hand takes is a hand-position difference, not a movement difference.

## Scale (#241)
The table below was scaled by the published 1800 mm. The demo's lifter says the machine is taller: seated at 0:25 his head reaches 71% of the machine's height, his shoulders 55% and the pad's top 60%, and standing at 0:21, nearer the camera, he still comes well short of its top. At 1800 mm his seated head would be 1280 mm up and he would be about 1.55 m tall. The sister HS-PEC is published at 206 cm. The kit is built to the lifter, since that is what a body has to fit: 2000 mm, every number scaled from the photo taken about 1.11 times the table's, and the mannequin then lands on the same three shares. The page's 1245 mm fits from the tower's back to the arms swept forward, not a base that long: the kit is 1300 mm that way, and 1560 mm wide with the arms out against the page's 1420.

## Motion
- Left arm and right arm: each revolute about a vertical axis in the overhead unit, mirrored left and right, sweeping in a horizontal plane. The axis is marked with a red indicator on the placard (Life Fitness page).
- The arms move together in the demo (0:34 to 0:40, both sweep out at once). The page does not say whether they are linked or independent; the one-arm rear-delt fly needs one arm to stay at rest, so treat them as independent levers on one stack (unverified).
- Each drop arm hangs from its lever on a horizontal pin at the lever's end (the round cap in the studio photo); the page says the arms pivot to fit users, so the drop can swing a little fore and aft. Its range is not shown.
- Range of motion, pec fly: about 100 degrees per arm, from the hands level with the shoulders and a little behind them (demo 0:25) to the hands nearly touching in front of the chest (demo 0:28). Estimated from those frames and the `pec_deck` video at 2:42 and 2:46; the views are oblique, so give or take 15 degrees.
- Range of motion, rear delt: about 55 degrees per arm on this machine, from the levers about 45 degrees behind the shoulder line, past the sides of the tower, the hands well ahead of the chest and about 1 m apart (demo 0:35) to the arms straight out to the sides in line with the shoulders (demo 0:40). The levers can't pass the tower, so the hands never come together here. The `reverse_pec_deck` video (10:26 to 10:28) shows hands together on another machine.
- Start position: a knob on the pivot unit picks one of five start angles (demo 0:14 to 0:18; page: five-position start). The angle between the positions is not shown; chosen 15 degrees, low confidence. The recipe uses one start for each exercise, the arms a little behind the shoulder line for the pec fly and 45 degrees behind it for the rear delt, so the step only places the other three.
- Both arms pull the one 138 kg stack through cable and pulleys (11 cm and 15 cm pulleys, page).

## Handles
- Each drop arm has a vertical grip and a short level grip on its other side, both square to the lever (studio photo; demo 0:25, 0:28). The vertical grip hangs like a cane from a bend at its top, its foot free, about 100 mm off the drop arm: ahead of it with the arms out, inward with them together, so the hand stays an arm's length from the shoulder. The pec fly holds the vertical grips (0:25). The rear delt holds the short ones, overhand: at 0:40 the hand sits on the pad's side of the drop arm and the vertical grip stands on the far side.

## Adjustments and fit rules
- Seat: height by a lever under the seat front, roller mechanism (demo 0:10, page).
- Fit, as the demo shows it: set the seat so the grips are level with the shoulders. The arms stay at shoulder height through both exercises.
- Pec fly: back on the pad; hands on the vertical grips, elbows slightly bent; the hands start level with the shoulders or a little behind and meet in front of the chest (demo 0:25 to 0:28; `pec_deck` video 2:42 to 2:46).
- Rear delt: straddle the seat facing the pad, chest on it, knees either side of the tower, arms forward to the short grips; sweep the arms out until they are in line with the shoulders (demo 0:35 to 0:40).
- Red axis indicator: the page says it marks the axis of rotation. The demo shows the shafts above the user's shoulders in both exercises: the rear delt user sits on the same seat, on the same side of the pad, turned to face it (0:35, 0:40).
- Built-in foot bar: on the HS-PEC only. The HS-FLY has none (studio photo, demo); the user's feet are on the floor.

## Open gaps
- Seat range and the start positions' angles: no frame shows the seat at its ends or the knob's steps, so both are chosen, low confidence.
- The lever radius comes from the published width, not a frame. The demo has no view from above or straight from the front.
- How far the rear beam runs out (420 each side, chosen) and whether it runs both ways: only the near side shows.
- The two rests stand 35 degrees apart (0:25 and 0:35) though the page says nothing is adjusted between the exercises: either the knob is moved off camera or an arm can swing back past its stop. The kit gives each exercise its own rest.
- The published 1800 mm height against the 2000 mm the demo's lifter gives (Scale). No page explains it; the demo's red-framed machine may be an earlier build.
- The levers stay straight and level, and the drop arms round; the real ones slope a little and are flat bars. Neither changes how the machine fits a body.
- The mannequin's arms stay more bent than the demo's, about 58 degrees at the elbow at the rear delt's end. They lie level, as the demo's do, but a straighter arm needs a lever longer than the footprint allows.
- Whether the arms are linked is not settled by any source.
