# Donkey calf machine (`donkey_calf`)

## Movement and exercises served
Ankle plantar flexion under a hip pad on a lever, loaded by a stack, with the trunk hinged forward onto a front rest. Serves `donkey_calf_raise` (Gastrocnemius, Soleus; pause 1 to 2 seconds at the bottom and roll the ankle on the balls of the feet).

The reference model and the technique video show the same machine, so the video settles sizes as well as the range of motion.

## Key dimensions (mm)
D is the distance forward of the base's rearmost point, Z the height above the floor, Y across from the machine's centre. Forward is toward the rest.

The video's positions come from a camera solve per frame, not a flat px scale, because the machine recedes. Vanishing points from the near rail and the cross members give the lens (995 px at 0:00.2, 1070 px at 0:09.0 and 0:11.4) and the camera height, and each point is then placed on the near side's plane or the centre plane. The scale is the published 1473 mm height, taken as the guide rod tops, the highest point. Below, "the solve" means this, scaled by that height. No body height is used.

Checks on the solve: the rail spacing comes out 749 mm inside-to-outside, so about 800 outside, against 787 published. The rail's thickness comes out 45 to 50, matching a 2 in tube. Plate pitch comes to 26.5 mm, a plausible 1 in plate plus a gap. Handle tips land 2090 to 2140 from the rear against 2210 published, 3 to 5% short, so the front end is the weakest part. If 1473 were the upright tops instead, spacing and length would both come out 10% over, so the rods it is. The lifter reaches about 1.60 m at the head while leaning forward with a bent leg at 0:00.2, which fits a person of about 1.70 to 1.75 m.

## Motion
- Arc, not a slide: the lever turns about the fixed pivot. Its rear end holds its place in the frame at 0:00.2, 0:09.0 and 0:11.4 once the camera's shift is taken out.
- Lever angle from the arm, estimated from the frames named, scaled by the solve (the angle doesn't change with the near arm's assumed Y): level at rest (video 0:00.2, -0.4 degrees), 8.8 degrees at the stretch (0:11.4), 14.3 degrees at the top (0:09.0). So 5.5 degrees in the rep, ±1. The rep never lets the lever back down to rest.
- Hip pad centre on that arc (D, Z), taking the pad as fixed to the lever: rest (1135, 1045), stretch (1137, 1153), top (1133, 1223). That is 70 mm of rise, nearly straight up, on a radius of about 740. The loop's front moved 85 mm between the same frames. The ankle rose 73 mm, which agrees.
- The pad swings on its hinge, a few degrees behind the lever: about 8 degrees at the stretch and 11 at the top, estimated from the pad's near edge in video 0:11.4 and 0:09.0, rough.
- Stack drive is direct, with no cable: a post stands on the stack's top plate and a cross axle at its head carries two black rollers that run under the lever's arms (video 0:09.0, 0:11.4; photo IMG_7766; no cable or pulley in the parts list). As the lever rises the rollers lift the post, and the rollers slide a little along the arms as the post stays upright. The post rides the two guide rods. For the recipe, stack rise = 390 x tan(lever angle), the post being 390 from the pivot.
- Stack travel: only the plates above the pin rise. In the video that is a block of about 95 (top plate and two plates), with a gap opening under it. Gap 93 to 103 at the top (0:09.0, 27 to 30 px), 31 to 41 at the stretch (0:11.4, 9 to 12 px), estimated from those frames, scaled by the solve. The lever's angle predicts 99 and 60: the top agrees, the stretch doesn't, by about 20. Use the formula.
- Leverage (chosen from the geometry, low confidence): the pad is about 1.9 times as far from the pivot as the post, so the pad carries about half the stack.
- Timing, from video 0:07 to 0:18: about 3 seconds a rep, with a pause of about 0.5 to 1 second at the stretch.

- Feet: close and parallel, toes straight forward, toe tips about 220 apart (video 0:09.0, the solve), so about hip width or a little under. The solve puts them about 75 off centre toward the far side, which is within its error; centre them. Balls of the feet on the bar with the toes about 30 to 60 over its front edge, the arch and heel hanging off the rear, about 170 clear of the stack. The ball sits over the bar's middle at the top and rolls to its rear edge at the stretch (D 1269 to 1219), which is the note's "rolling your ankle".
- Knees: soft at the top, about 10 degrees bent, bending to about 30 at the stretch as the shin leans forward.
- Hips: the trunk sits 19 to 21 degrees above level, shoulders higher than the hips, at both ends of the rep. The hip angle between trunk and thigh is about 105 degrees at the top and 98 at the stretch. The thigh runs 3 degrees off vertical at the top and 13 at the stretch, knee forward.
- Pad on the body: across the low back and the top of the pelvis, above the hip joint, with its front edge on the shirt's lower back and its rear over the top of the glutes (video 0:09.0). Not on the shoulders. Global Fitness's text says across the hips.
- Forearms and hands: elbows on the rest near its rear edge, upper arms near vertical, forearms running forward along the pad, hands around the handles at about mid-length, a neutral grip with the palms in. Elbows and hands hold still through the rep; the body rocks about them.
- Head: hanging forward of the shoulders, neck bent, face down over the forearms, a little lower at the top than at the stretch (head top Z 1072, 1116).
- Getting in: step onto the bar (0:01), hands on the handles, knees bent to get under the level pad (0:02.2), then straighten the legs to lift it (0:03 to 0:05).

## Adjustments and fit rules
- Stack pin: the only adjustment seen in use.
- Lever height: a knob on the post, just below the arms (video 0:09.0, catalog picture) and a 3 in pop pin in the parts list point to a pin that sets the post's length and so the lever's height for the user. The video never shows it moved. Unverified, medium confidence.
- The rest, foot bar and handles are fixed.
- Fit, as the video shows it: balls of the feet on the bar, forearms on the rest, hands on the handles, pad on the low back above the hip joint, knees soft.

## Open gaps
- Built differently from the numbers above, to fit the mannequin. The camera solve reads this lifter's hip to ankle at about 670, where a 1.75 m lifter's legs are about 850, so the body's places above don't fit the mannequin. The machine's sizes stand, and these move:
  - The lever turns from 8.7 to 17.6 degrees, not 8.8 to 14.3, so the pad rises about 124, not 70. A leg of the mannequin's length needs that for the knees to hold still, and the ankles to do the work.
  - The hip pad's reach is 800, not 735, at the edge of its +-60, which puts the elbows at the rest's rear and the hands past its front.
  - The pad is fixed to the lever, without its hinge, and meets the top of the pelvis.
  - The rest's top is 905, not 870, and the rest legs run 40 higher, for the forearms to lie on it.
  - The trunk lies about 6 to 14 degrees above level, not 20, for the elbows to reach the rest, and pivots on the shoulders: the elbows move about 55 for the hips' 129.
  - The knees hold about 168 at both ends, not 170 and 150. The sole's range is the video's.
  - The wrists bend 40 degrees toward the little finger, set by the pose, so the forearms lie level while the hands follow the sloping handles.
  - The stack is the library's, 12 plates, not 20.
- Trusted least: the hip pad's fore-and-aft place and depth (centre D 1135, ±60, 230 deep). It is black on black, edge-on at 0:00.2 and half hidden by the lifter in every loaded frame; its front edge comes from a soft corner in one frame, and the depth from a product picture's proportions. The catalog picture seems to put the pad's front past the loop's front, while the solve puts it about 30 behind.
- The rest pad's width (about 890) is wider than the published 787, so the published width must be the frame's, or the solve is wrong here. The front photo agrees with the solve.
- The solve's handle tips land 70 to 120 short of the published length. Let the difference go in the rest legs if the kit must reach 2210 exactly.
- No maker's document. Every published size is a dealer's, 615 rests on one parts seller, and Global Fitness, Gym Equipment Inc and eBay refused the fetch.
- The stack's rise at the stretch disagrees by about 20 between the gap and the lever's angle; the plan size of the stack is rough, and the plates' 26.5 pitch with 20 lb a plate would want a smaller plan than 330 x 220, so the depth may be nearer 150.
- For the mannequin, chosen from the fit rule, low confidence: balls of the feet centred on the bar at D 1265, Z 155, feet 220 apart; forearms on the rest at Z 870; solve the lever's angle so the pad's underside meets the low back at the top and the stretch, keeping the 5.5 degree swing and 50 degree ankle range, and let the knees and hips take up what is left. The lifter's angles above come from a body about 1.70 to 1.75 m tall; the mannequin may need a different lever height, which the post's pin would allow.
- The pad's hinge travel, the roller head's detail and the floor cross member's section are read by eye. Treat the pad as fixed to the lever if the small swing isn't worth a joint.
