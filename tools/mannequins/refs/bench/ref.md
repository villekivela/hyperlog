# Adjustable bench (`bench`)

A bench of a short seat and a long back pad, either side of a hinge, on a spine with a raked leg at each end. Two arcs of holes hang from the pads and pin to the frame to set each pad's angle.

## Movement and exercises served
The pads don't move through the rep: each is set before the set and stays there. The back pad sets the exercise (flat, incline, upright); the seat tilts up to stop the lifter sliding. With the decline kit the lifter lies the other way round, the ankles held at the back pad's high end and the head down toward the seat.

Posed on it today: barbell_bench_press, close_grip_bench_press, db_incline_press (every baked angle), cable_shoulder_press (fixed, upright), barbell_hip_thrust (across the body).

The library's adjustable exercises, baked at every angle once posed (the two decline ones at the decline setting): arnold_press_seated, barbell_incline_press, chest_supported_db_row, db_incline_curl, db_incline_press, decline_bench_press, decline_db_press, incline_chest_supported_db_row, incline_db_kelso_shrug, incline_db_y_raise, seated_db_shoulder_press, seated_overhead_press_barbell, smith_machine_incline_press, smith_machine_seated_press.

## Motion
- Back pad: revolute about the hinge. 9 positions. Rogue's page says "from flat bench to 85 degrees upright"; its chart draws flat, 8, 18, 28, 38, 48, 58, 68 and 78 degrees, which is nine, and the 78 line lies on the drawn pad's face (measured 78.2 degrees). The bench goes to 85, the owner's call, 2026-11-04 (#195); see Open gaps.
- Seat: revolute about the hinge boss. 6 positions. The chart draws 40, 30, 20, 10, flat and -15 degrees.
- Decline: -15 degrees, the chart's figure, taken as the kit's by the owner (#195); see Open gaps. The kit rides on the back pad and turns with it: a square post telescopes out of the pad's frame tube at the head end, held by a pop pin in the side of the frame, and ends in a Y into a cross tube with a pair of rollers at each end (the owner's screenshot). In the fitted photo the pad is raised, the seat stays flat, the near pair sits just past the pad's end at about the height of its face, and the far pair is further out and lower, the cross tube bent about 50 degrees toward the pad's underside (the two photos together: the far pair is the larger in the screenshot, taken from behind). The knees go over the near pair and the ankles hook behind the far one.
- The wheels are at the head end and the handle at the seat end: lift the handle and the bench rolls on them. The handle hangs on a plate under the seat's front; the photos show it hanging down, the drawings swung up about 16 degrees below level.

## Adjustments and fit rules
No placard. The AB-2 gives 54 seat and back combinations ("more than 50" on the page). Each pad is held by a pop pin through its arc, one in the spine for the back and one in the front leg for the seat. For the mannequin: the hips against the seat-back corner, the back along the pad. The gap falls under the lumbar arch on a flat press, and a review on the page puts a 6 ft lifter's seat at its edge with the head at the pad's top.

## Open gaps
- **The top angle.** The page says 85 degrees; the maker's chart stops at 78, and that is measured on the drawn pad as well as labelled. No source shows an 85 degree position. The bench goes to 85 because the owner chose it.
- **The bench's steps.** The maker's positions are 0, 8, 18 and on in tens; the clips are baked at 0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75 and 85, and the phone turns the pad to the exact angle.
- **Decline.** -15 degrees is drawn on the SEAT PAD chart, on the seat, and the chart shows no kit, so calling it the kit's figure is a guess (the owner's "probably"). The AB-3 pages give no decline angle. The fitted photo points another way: the back pad above flat and the lifter reversed, so the decline would be the pad's own raised angle, whatever hole it's in. It is a three-quarter view, so no angle was read from it.
- The kit's own page didn't load. Its post, its roller sizes, the cross tube's lie against the pad and how far the post telescopes come from photos only.
- The chart and the PDF disagree on the seat's thickness and its height against the back pad, by about 13 mm. The PDF is taken, as the photos match it.
- The pad turning about a point near it rather than the boss is worked out from one drawing, and the drawing may be a composite. The boss's height is good; the working pivot is rough.
- The pad's width at the seat isn't published. The seat is taken as the back pad's 305.

## As built (#195)
`recipes/bench.mjs` follows this file, and departs where a number or a photo was missing:
- The back pad turns about a hinge on its underside, 57 mm below its top and 41.8 cm behind the seat's front, within the +-20 of the chart's working pivot. The boss 86 mm under the pad's top is a fixed part: turning the pad about it would lay the pad over the seat, which the chart doesn't show.
- The back arc has ten slots, the chart's nine and one more for the 85 degrees, and its radius is 320 about that hinge, so the two arcs stay apart as the drawings have them. In the studio both pads snap to their slots (owner, 2026-11-04); the clips stay at their baked angles, and the phone turns the pad to the exact angle.
- Each side of the base is three lengths of tube, two legs and the spine, cut to meet on sharp mitres with a seam at each corner (owner, 2026-11-04), each leg butted into the middle of its foot's top (owner, 2026-11-04), the foot's ends closed by the elevation's angled gussets.
- The seat tilts on a joint of its own from -15 to 40 degrees, with its frame, its arc and the handle, about the hinge boss, its six slots where the front leg's pin lands at each of the chart's settings (owner, 2026-11-04). It is flat unless a pose sets `seat`, and the phone doesn't turn it. The poses assume a flat seat: one that tilts it needs its hips solved on the tilted seat.
- The handle hangs straight down, as the photos show it.
- The decline kit is the `decline` option on the back pad, the foot catch, with the post at full stretch and running straight to the cross tube, with no Y, and only a decline movement's entry sets it (owner, 2026-11-04). It stands the pad at 15 degrees, the size of the chart's -15, the lifter reversed, and such a pose bakes at that one angle. None is posed yet, so no clip shows it. The cross tube's 50 degrees and the rollers' sizes are the estimates above.
- `height` still lowers a bench for a pose that needs it: the hip thrust's stands at 392 mm, not the reference's 457.
- Sizes fitted to the published footprint and tube sizes: the feet stand 1129 apart, the twin tubes are 157 over both, and the back pad's frame is 871 x 51.
- The pads have no two-tone edging, and no brand, logo or decal.
