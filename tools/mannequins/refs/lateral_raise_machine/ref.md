# Lateral raise machine (`lateral_raise_machine`)

## Movement and exercises served
Seated shoulder abduction: the user sits facing the machine with the chest on a centre pad, the elbows bent, the outside of each upper arm under a pad on a lever arm whose pivot lines up with the shoulder joint. The arms rise out to the sides to horizontal, loaded by one weight stack.
- `machine_lateral_raise`: seated, as above (Hammer Strength demo; technique video).
- `standing_machine_lateral_raise`: its video shows a different machine, a standing plate-loaded one (Atlantis): the user stands beside a lever whose pivot sits in front of the shoulder at shoulder height and raises it with a straight arm by a curved handle. This seated machine cannot be used standing: the seat and chest pad are in the way and the pivots are at seated shoulder height (about 1.1 m). `standing_machine_lateral_raise` uses the standing machine's geometry (Motion, last bullet): its own pose, the pivot in front of the standing shoulder at shoulder height and about 75 degrees of straight-arm travel. This kit is not reused with the seat ignored, because its pivots sit at seated shoulder height and its bent-arm pads do not match the straight-arm lift in the video.

## Motion
- Left arm and right arm: each revolute about a horizontal axis pointing front to back, through the red dot (the page marks the axis of rotation with a red indicator). Each lever is a dog-leg: from the pivot out and down, then back in under the arm to the pad, so at rest the pad hangs below the pivot (hero photo).
- Range of motion: about 80 degrees per arm, from the upper arms hanging about 10 degrees out from the sides (pads below the pivots) to the upper arms horizontal, pads on top of the arms. Estimated from the demo at 0:08 and 0:09 and the technique video at 0:02 and 0:09, where at the top the elbows reach shoulder height or a little above; give or take 10 degrees.
- The two arms rise together in both videos; the page does not say whether they are linked.
- Both arms load the one 95 kg stack through cable and pulleys (11 cm and 15 cm pulleys, per the page).
- Each grip pivots to fit users (page text). It hangs inboard of the pad and ahead of it, in line with the upper arm (hero photo), and the hands hold it with the elbows bent about 90 degrees (demo 0:09, 0:15). The kit hangs it off the lever's knee, 170 mm ahead of the pad's front end, where the mannequin's forearm reaches.
- Standing machine (the `standing_machine_lateral_raise` video, not this machine): one lever per side, its pivot in front of the standing user's shoulder at shoulder height, a curved handle at about arm's length. The straight arm rises from the hand by the hip (7:08, 7:19) to about horizontal (7:06): about 75 degrees.

## Handles
- One grip ahead of each arm pad, hanging inboard of it, pivoting (page). The elbows rest on the pads and the hands hold the grips; the pads, not the hands, take the load.

## Adjustments and fit rules
- Seat height by a lever under the seat front, roller mechanism (demo 0:01 to 0:03, page).
- Fit rule, checked: the pivots are fixed and the seat moves, so the seat sets the shoulder to the pivot. In the demo at 0:08 the user's shoulder joint is level with the red dot (dot at 282 px, shoulder at about 280 px), seen from behind. The pivot does not move with the seat.
- Chest on the centre pad, the outside of the upper arms under the arm pads near the elbows (demo 0:08, 0:19).
- Pad positions are optimised for the middle deltoid (fitshop page).

## Open gaps
- Seat range: no frame shows the seat at its ends, so it is chosen around the measured seat, low confidence.
- The pivots stand at 1.15 m and the seat at 0.56 m for the mannequin, above the table's estimates (1.05 to 1.10 m, about 0.48 m), from their shares of the tower in the demo; the shoulder stays on the pivot.
- The pivot spacing is rough (one oblique photo). A spacing narrower than the shoulders means the pads slide a little on the arms through the arc; the recipe sets the spacing to the mannequin's shoulders instead (#212: 0.192 m out each side).
- The standing exercise has no reference model; its geometry comes from one video of two other makes. It needs a kit of its own, so it stays unposed: the owner's call (#241).
- Left plain: a square tower, 17% narrower than the real rounded one (the shared stack frame), an even stack where the real one is narrow over wide, round pads where the real ones are D sections. The pivots stand wider than the photo's, on the mannequin's shoulders, which is what keeps the pads on the arms.
